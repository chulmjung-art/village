# Joseon Court Dress: Who Wears What

Our reference for dressing every character in the village, so each role reads at a glance and
nobody wears the wrong hat. Researched 2026-11-20. Where sources disagree, it says so.

## The big rule: the hat tells you the rank

| Hat | Who | How to recognise it |
|---|---|---|
| **익선관 ikseongwan** | King and crown prince, everyday | Black, two tiers (the back tier taller), with **two small wings standing straight UP at the back**, like a cicada's wings. The upright wings mark the king's high place. |
| **사모 samo** | Officials (civil and military) on duty | Black, rounded, with **two wide, flat wings sticking out to the SIDES**. In late Joseon the crown was high and the wings short and level. |
| **면류관 myeonryugwan** | King, for the biggest rites only (ancestral rites, weddings) | A flat board on top with **strings of beads hanging front and back**. The number of strings shows rank: 9 for a king, 12 for an emperor. |
| **원유관 wonyugwan** | King and crown prince, at morning court, New Year, receiving envoys | A tall ridged crown with jewelled pins. |
| **갓 gat** | Scholars (seonbi) and gentlemen off duty | Wide round brim of fine black horsehair or bamboo, see-through, with a tall crown. |
| **전립 jeollip** | Soldiers, guards, constables | A felt hat with a round brim, often with a plume or tassel. |
| **초립 chorip** | Young men, and some guards on duty | A straw hat, smaller than the gat. |

**The mistake in our first drafts:** the King's hat came out with wide side wings. That's the
official's **samo**, not the king's **ikseongwan**. The fix: the King's wings must **stand up
at the back**, not stick out to the sides.

## Royal family

| Who | Everyday dress | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **King (왕)**, our director | **Red** dragon robe (곤룡포 gonryongpo) with **five-clawed** gold dragon roundels on the chest, back and both shoulders; a jade belt (옥대); black boots; the **ikseongwan** | For grand rites he'd switch to the myeonbok (면복) and myeonryugwan, but everyday court life is the red robe. |
| **Emperor (황제)**, only after 1897 | **Yellow** dragon robe (황룡포) | Joseon rulers were kings, not emperors. Gojong only declared the Korean Empire in 1897. The red robe then passed to the crown prince and princes. |
| **Crown prince (세자)** | Dragon robe with **four-clawed** dragons, also with the ikseongwan | Sources disagree on the colour: most say **dark blue (아청)**, one academic paper says black. |
| **Queen (왕비)** | **Red** for her ceremonial robe (원삼 wonsam / 적의 jeogui, the pheasant robe); everyday a 당의 dangui (short ceremonial jacket) with gold-leaf patterns (금박) over a long skirt | Royal dangui have gold leaf. Court ladies' dangui don't. |
| **Crown princess (세자빈)** | Wonsam in **purple** | Some sources say purple-red, or green and purple. |
| **Princess (공주/옹주)** | Wonsam in **green** | |

## Officials (관리)

There are three types of official dress:
- **Sangbok (상복):** everyday duty dress. A **black** round-collared robe (흑단령), the samo, a rank belt (품대) and boots (화). Use this for officials in the village day to day.
- **Sibok (시복):** a **red** round-collared robe for official events.
- **Jobok (조복) / gongbok (공복):** full court dress for attending court and great ceremonies.

**Rank badge (흉배)**, the embroidered square on the chest and back. From King Jeongjo's
time until 1910:
- **Civil officials:** cranes. **Two cranes** for ranks 1 to 3 (senior ranks), **one crane** for ranks 4 to 9.
- **Military officials:** tigers. **Two tigers** for senior ranks, **one tiger** for junior ranks.

**Robe colour by rank** (ceremonial robes). Sources agree that ranks 1 to 3 wear **red**.
Below that they disagree: one says blue for ranks 3 to 6 and green for ranks 7 to 9, another
says blue for ranks 4 to 9.

## Palace staff and guards

| Who | Dress | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Eunuchs (내시)** | Official dress, because they held official ranks in the eunuch bureau (내시부). Shown in portraits wearing a dallyeong robe and a hat. | The horned hat eunuchs wear in modern TV dramas is said to be anachronistic. The only men besides the royal family allowed to stay in the palace overnight. |
| **Senior court ladies (상궁)** | **Green** dangui at royal events, with hair up in a garima wrap | Junior court ladies: pink or yellow-green jeogori jacket with a navy skirt. Court lady wonsam came in green, blue and black. |
| **Palace guards (무예청 and others)** | On duty: the celestial robe (천익), a straw hat (초립) and a "tiger beard" plume (호수), by a 1778 regulation | |
| **Soldiers and constables (포졸)** | Military dress (군복): the 철릭 cheollik (a long robe with a pleated skirt for movement) or a sleeveless vest, with the **jeollip** felt hat | Early Joseon: cheollik plus a hat with a plume. Later the cheollik got wider and less practical. |

## Townsfolk (for our town characters)

| Who | Dress |
|---|---|
| **Scholar (선비)** | Plain **white or pale jade 도포 dopo** (a long overcoat with a split back flap), a sash, and the black **gat** |
| **Gentleman off duty** | Dopo or a simpler 두루마기 durumagi coat, with the gat |
| **Commoner and craftsman** | Undyed white or earth-brown jeogori jacket and baji trousers, sleeves tied back for work, a headband or straw hat |

## How this maps to our village roles

| Our role | Wear |
|---|---|
| **King / Chief Human Officer** (the director) | Red gonryongpo, five-clawed gold dragon roundels on the chest, back and shoulders, jade belt, black boots, the **ikseongwan with upright back wings**, plus his glasses |
| **영의정 Chief State Councillor** (palace) | The highest civil official (senior 1st rank): **black sangbok dallyeong**, the samo with flat side wings, a **double-crane** badge, a rank belt |
| **사또 Town magistrate** | A mid-rank civil official: black dallyeong, the samo, a **single-crane** badge |
| **The six local clerks (이방, 호방 and the rest)** | Local clerks (아전), not court officials: plain dark robes, a black gat or a simple clerk's hat, **no rank badge** *(sources thin; checking first)* |
| **선비 and scholars** (writer, translator, historian, astronomer) | White or jade dopo with the black gat |
| **Craftsmen** (blacksmith, carpenter, painter) | Work jeogori and baji, sleeves tied up, a headband |
| **포졸 constables and guards** | Military cheollik or vest, the jeollip felt hat, a spear |
| **파발 riders** | Cheollik riding dress, the jeollip, on horseback |
| **암행어사 secret inspector** | A plain scholar's dopo and gat. Looks like anyone until he reveals himself. |
| **Household helper** | Commoner jeogori and chima (or baji), undyed white or earth tones |
